## Multiple-outage cohorts

The two-outage cohort was selected before outcomes using seed `20261017`: 10 models
without replacement from each of the 30- and 60-activity strata. A distinct eligible
worker receives a second outage in the first outage's interval. Eligibility requires
positive overlap with an original duty and no duty started before onset that remains
running at onset. Seeded selection chooses among eligible workers. Ineligible or
unproved events are retained rather than replaced. The frozen 20-model list and eligible
workers are in [evidence/multioutage_protocol.json](evidence/multioutage_protocol.json).
The 14 complete `k=2` cases supply the affected-pair extension: 40,542 queries, of which
19,830 are compatible, 20,701 incompatible, and 11 unresolved. A query protects the
flexible set minus the affected pair. Records are in `supplement/v27_pair_compatibility.json`
and `supplement/k2_requests/` inside the saved-results archive.

The Poisson protocol uses seed `2026102707`, sampling 5 models from each size stratum
(30 and 60 activities), with 10 events per model. A homogeneous Poisson process has
mean 2 arrivals in `[0.42 T0, 0.58 T0)`; arrival times are rounded to integers. Distinct
eligible workers are selected with seeded randomness. Outage duration is
`max(1, round(0.08 T0))`, and the deadline is `ceil(1.5 T0)`. Duties started before the
first arrival are frozen. Zero-arrival, ineligible and unproved events are retained.
The archive's `supplement/v23_poisson_protocol.json` records model choices and generated
events; `supplement/v23_poisson_summary.json` records outcomes. Of 100 slots, 95 have
proved optima and 85 complete maps; 35 complete maps have `k>=2`. Three seeded requests
at capacity and three above capacity per such map give 105 per category: 54 at-capacity
and 105 above-capacity requests are infeasible. These constructed cohorts test joint
compatibility, rather than estimate empirical outage frequencies.

## Random policy and uncertainty

The main matched experiment has one deterministic seeded Random trajectory per model.
Integer weights from 1 to 10 are derived from seed `2026102601`, model ID, day and the
`joint_random` key. The same weight vector is used in the request and no-request arms.
The solver uses one worker and seed 41. Five independent Random sequences belong to
the separate earlier no-request experiment, not to the main 20 s matched experiment.
The main comparison uses model-level, size-stratified bootstrap intervals (10,000
resamples). These intervals are exploratory and unadjusted for multiple comparisons.
The 977-day sensitivity recount filters original trajectories; it does not rerun
histories after removing fallback days.

## Requester and non-requester burden

Run `python -B archival_checks.py` from `versions/v37`. Outputs go to
`reproduced_archival/`. No optimization is performed. The script replays each policy's
saved affected sets and asserts the recorded history updates. Within each model, one
fixed group contains every worker ever included in the archived History requests over
20 days; the other contains workers never included. One model has no member of the
second group, so comparisons use the remaining 49 models. Outage workers do not accrue
impact on their outage day. Both arms use the same History-selected realized daily C/G.

| Policy | Maximum among ever-requested, no constraints → enforced | Maximum among never-requested, no constraints → enforced | Total impacts among never-requested, no constraints → enforced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | 7.86 → 4.22 | 2.76 → 5.80 | 10.35 → 17.82 |
| Random | 3.37 → 3.02 | 3.49 → 3.53 | 21.55 → 22.45 |
| Round-robin | 3.04 → 2.67 | 2.92 → 3.16 | 22.06 → 23.12 |
| Least-recent | 2.98 → 2.90 | 2.65 → 2.84 | 21.86 → 22.33 |
| Burden-aware | 2.94 → 2.78 | 2.71 → 2.71 | 21.37 → 22.08 |

Entries are means of model-level group summaries in days/counts. The unchanged mean
maximum for Burden-aware does not imply unchanged burdens for every worker: total
impact among never-requested workers rises. Group membership derives from First-based
request histories; this is a descriptive matched-budget analysis, not a causal or
human-fairness estimate. Full precision and model-level rows are regenerated by the script.

## Sampling-first additive cost estimate

The same script uses the four-event cost pilot in `supplement/v19_projection.json.gz`.
For each matched prefix it adds recorded Exact request times only at Sample-20 misses
to Sample-20's recorded complete session time. Common preparation is counted once.
The following values are medians across the four fixed events, in seconds.

| Requests | Exact recorded total | Sample-20 recorded total | Sample-20 plus Exact-at-misses additive charge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1.274 | 1.472 | 1.487 |
| 5 | 1.275 | 1.474 | 1.531 |
| 20 | 1.296 | 1.480 | 1.704 |

This is an archival cost estimate, not a timed hybrid implementation. Exact costs
retain the original Exact stream states; skipped queries could change hints and caches.
No additional Exact initialization is charged. The estimate does not establish a
general speed ranking. In this local pilot it provides no estimated advantage over
Exact alone. The four-event pilot is distinct from the 1,000 History requests and their
113 menu refusals; their counts must not be combined.

## Supporting policy and offline-bound records

Separate no-request experiments report Minimax/Burden-aware mean maximum impact
3.34/3.32, with 49/50 model ties. Count-first uses 2+2 s and enters stage 2 only after
proving the count optimum; its count/shift is 3.38/60.52 against 3.32/81.84 for
Burden-aware. These limits differ from the main 20 s matched experiment.

For the archived 4 s Burden-aware runs, offline maximum 20-day impact-count bounds
certify equality for 20 of 25 small models. Mean excess lies in [0.16, 0.24]. A finite
feasible-column restricted master supplies an upper bound; the separate valid global
relaxation supplies the lower bound. These are solver bounds, not a claim that a finite
restricted master alone proves the unrestricted optimum. Original records and
verification scope remain in the saved-results archive.

## Refusal evidence

Solver-confirmed refusal, replayed assumption-core diagnostics and independently
checked arithmetic obstructions provide different guarantees. The 99 base refusals
have replayed cores; 86 are subset-minimal and 63 contain only the request. Independent
arithmetic obstructions cover 32 (30 Hall, 2 temporal); the other 67 retain solver
statuses and core diagnostics. This 32/99 coverage is not the coverage of the 113
History-menu refusals. No external UNSAT proof trace is supplied.



## Frozen design details

Run `python -B verify_design_details.py` from `versions/v37`. It checks saved records
without optimization. The design details below supplement the summaries above.

### Workday event screening

The shared 50-model, 20-day cohort uses lists of 20 candidates per day, frozen before
production solving. Each candidate samples an original positive-duration activity and
assigned worker; its outage begins at that activity's original start. The first candidate
whose lexicographic activity and participant optima are both proved is accepted. These
events are then reused by all policies before generating protection requests. Screening
uses stability proof completion, not later request delivery or burden outcomes.

Across 1,000 accepted events, 1,012 candidates were attempted: 12 attempts failed to prove
the activity-change optimum C*, causing replacements on 8 days. All 12 have status
`C_UNPROVED` (4 C-stage FEASIBLE, 8 UNKNOWN); the G stage was not reached. Thus 12 is
the number of rejected attempts, not the number of replaced days. The frozen screening
extract, relative source names and SHA-256 records are in
`docs/evidence/frozen_workday_screening.json`. The public matched records independently
confirm 8 days with candidate index greater than zero and 12 skipped candidate positions.

### Baseline objectives

Random minimizes a sum of seeded integer weights from 1 to 10 over affected non-outage
workers. It is not uniform sampling from feasible schedules or affected sets.

Least-recent stores a last-impact date `last[w]`, initialized to -1. For n workers with
zero-based IDs, its weight is `(last[w]+1)*(n*n+1)+w`; the current outage worker has
weight zero. It minimizes the sum of these weights over affected workers and updates
dates for all adopted non-outage impacts. At fixed actual G and fixed outage flags,
this is equivalent to first minimizing summed last-impact dates, then summed worker
IDs. The multiplier makes the date sum dominate the ID sum. The checker verifies all
2,000 matched-arm Least-recent weight vectors and date updates.

The 977-day recount uses the intersection of OPTIMAL statuses for Random, Round-robin,
Least-recent and Burden-aware. First is not part of the optimality criterion. Recounting
filters the original trajectories and preserves their earlier histories and fallbacks.

### Compatibility denominators and diagnostic interventions

Protection capacity `|F|-k` counts flexible workers only. Safe workers are unaffected in
every strict optimum and do not consume this flexible capacity. The 35 complete Poisson
maps with k>=2 each contribute 3 seeded requests at capacity and 3 above capacity, giving
105 per category; 54 and 105, respectively, have saved INFEASIBLE statuses.

The pair extension exhausts all unordered flexible-worker pairs in the 14 k=2 maps
within the preselected 20-model two-outage cohort. The 40,542 pairs are queries within
14 model events, not independent scheduling scenarios. Each query protects F minus
that pair. Their totals are 19,830 compatible, 20,701 incompatible, 11 UNKNOWN.

In the 99 base-refusal diagnostics, removing C/G caps alone rescues 22. Removing both
caps and frozen start/crew equalities rescues 35 in total, including those 22. Both
interventions retain the original deadline and start lower bounds for originally
unstarted activities. Extending the deadline alone rescues 2. Unfreezing is a diagnostic
counterfactual, not an executable remedy. The checker recounts these saved status
categories; it does not generate external proofs of negative solver answers.
